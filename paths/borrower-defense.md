# Borrower defense

**Frank's first choice** (D-004). Status: **not filed, as far as this repo knows (NEED, Q-003).**

## What it is

A request to cancel federal loans because **the school misled you or broke the law** in a way
that affected your decision to enroll or borrow. Compared to PSLF: PSLF is earned over ten years
of work, like a pension vesting. Borrower defense is a claim, more like a refund for a product
that was misrepresented. If it is approved, the loans from that school are discharged, and
payments already made on them can be refunded.

It is not a hardship program. It only covers loans for the school in the claim (Q-004).

The University of Phoenix has been the subject of a large federal action:

- **Sweet v. Cardona**, a class action settled in 2022. The University of Phoenix was, as Claude
  remembers it, one of the schools listed in the settlement, which meant borrowers from those
  schools who had **already applied** by the settlement date got their loans discharged
  automatically. (Claude, from memory, verify at studentaid.gov.)

**So the first question is not "how strong is the claim," it is "did Frank ever apply, and
when."** If he applied in time, he may already be in a class. If not, a new application today
goes through the regular process, and the evidence and narrative matter.

## What the application needs

From memory, to verify against the form itself on studentaid.gov:

- The school, the dates of attendance, the program.
- **What the school told him** (recruiter promises, job placement claims, credit transfer, cost),
  **how he relied on it**, and **how it hurt him.**
- Any evidence: emails, enrollment agreement, ads, notes, transcripts.
- Signed under penalty of perjury. Every word must be his and true (D-002).
