# PSLF, Public Service Loan Forgiveness

**Frank's fallback** (D-004). Status: qualifying employer, qualifying plan, **count unknown
(NEED).**

## What it is

After **120 qualifying monthly payments** (ten years, need not be in a row) while working full time
for a qualifying employer, the remaining balance is forgiven, tax free. A Florida public school
district qualifies. Think of it like a pension vesting schedule: the clock only runs in months that
meet every condition at once.

A month counts when all of these are true:

1. **Direct Loan.** His is a Direct Consolidation Loan. Yes.
2. **Qualifying plan.** IBR qualifies. Graduated and extended plans do not, which Frank already
   noted himself.
3. **Full-time qualifying employment** that month. Yes, while at the district.
4. **Paid in full and on time**, or a $0 payment under an income-driven plan. A $0 payment still
   counts.

## What does not count

- **Months in deferment or forbearance.** So the WGU deferment pauses the clock. Frank accepted
  that cost (D-005).
- Months in default.

## Buyback, the thing that softens the deferment cost

From Claude's memory, to verify on studentaid.gov: there is a **PSLF buyback** that lets a borrower
pay for past months spent in deferment or forbearance, **if** they were working for a qualifying
employer during those months, and **if** buying them back would get them to 120. The amount is
roughly what an income-driven payment would have been for those months.

What that means for Frank: the WGU months are not necessarily lost forever. They might be bought
back near the end, but only if that is the thing standing between him and 120. This is not a
reason to undo D-005; it is a reason to keep records of the dates.

- Whether the Fresh Start and IDR account adjustment years ago credited old months. They may have.
