# The rules as of 2026, and how far to trust them

**Everything in this file is Claude's memory, as of mid 2026.** None of it has been checked
against studentaid.gov yet. Use it to know what to ask, not as a fact to act on. When a line is
verified, add the source and the date next to it.

## The 2025 law

A federal law signed in July 2025 rewrote student loan repayment. As Claude remembers it:

- A new plan, the **Repayment Assistance Plan (RAP)**, opened on 1 July 2026. It counts for PSLF.
- **SAVE is gone.** People on it were moved.
- **IBR stays available** to people already borrowing, which is Frank. (verify)
- Older plans (PAYE, ICR) are being phased out over the next couple of years.
- **In-school deferment still exists.** Some other deferments end for new loans, not existing ones.

## PSLF

- 120 qualifying payments, full-time qualifying employment, Direct Loans, qualifying plan.
- Deferment and forbearance months do not count, with buyback as the exception (`../paths/pslf.md`).
- Rules about which employers qualify were being revised in 2025 and 2026. A public school
  district is the clearest case of a qualifying employer, so this should not affect Frank. (verify)

## Borrower defense

- The rules for judging an application depend on when the loans were first disbursed and when the
  application was filed. Newer rules from 2022 were delayed. (verify)
- Sweet v. Cardona covered applications filed by certain dates in 2022. (verify)

## Words, in plain language

- **Deferment:** a pause on payments, here because Frank is in school.
- **Forbearance:** a pause on payments for other reasons. Interest usually keeps growing.
- **IDR / income-driven repayment:** a plan where the payment is set from income, not the balance.
  IBR is one.
- **Capitalization:** unpaid interest gets added to the balance, so future interest is charged on
  it too. Like a credit card adding last month's interest to this month's balance.
- **NSLDS:** the federal database of every federal loan. studentaid.gov shows it.
- **Servicer:** the company that runs the account (Aidvantage). It does not own the loan; the
  Department of Education does.
